What to do right now

  1. Don't leave for the airport on a guess

    Open your airline's app first. When the stop lifts, flights restart in waves and many will already be cancelled. Your airline's status beats any board in the terminal.

  2. Rebook in the app, not the line

    Most carriers issue a travel waiver during events like this, so changes are free. The app and the phone line reach the same seats as the gate agent, minus the queue.

  3. Cancelled or badly delayed? You can take the refund

    If your flight is cancelled, or delayed 3+ hours domestic or 6+ hours international, and you decide not to go, U.S. rules require a refund to your original payment. The cause doesn't matter. You don't have to accept a voucher. DOT refund rules

  4. Hotels and meals are a courtesy this time

    Airline hotel and meal promises cover problems the airline caused. An air traffic control outage isn't one. Ask anyway, and check the travel protection on the credit card you booked with. DOT airline commitments

  5. Stuck on the plane?

    Airlines must offer water and a snack by the two-hour mark of a tarmac delay and let you off by three hours on domestic flights, four on international, unless safety or security prevents it. DOT tarmac delay rules

What we know, in order

    Quick answers

    What is a ground stop?

    An FAA order that holds aircraft at their departure airport. Planes that haven't taken off stay put. Flights already in the air keep going and land.

    When will it end?

    The FAA has not given a time. The order says departures stay grounded "until further notice." This page flips the moment a lift is confirmed, and shows when the status was last checked.

    Has this happened before?

    Rarely. The last nationwide ground stop was in January 2023, when a failure in the system that distributes safety notices to pilots halted departures for about 90 minutes, delayed more than 10,000 flights and cancelled more than 1,000. Before that, September 11, 2001.

    Is it safe to fly once it lifts?

    The stop exists so that nothing takes off until controllers can talk to and track every aircraft. Flights resume only when the FAA says the system is working.

    My flight is tomorrow. Am I affected?

    Possibly. Aircraft and crews are out of position tonight, and it takes airlines a day or more to recover from a full stop. Watch your airline's app for schedule changes and waivers.
